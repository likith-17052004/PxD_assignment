District-level crop & rainfall explorer

India Crop Yield Explorer

A full-screen district map for comparing crop yield, sown area, and monsoon rainfall across India, side by side. Pick a crop, a season and a year on each side, then swipe one map over the other to see where things changed.

What this is

This tool puts three public datasets — a crop-production yearbook, IMD rainfall grids, and a district boundary map — on one map, so questions like "was this a weak monsoon year here?" or "how did sown area for this crop change between two years?" can be answered by looking, not by joining spreadsheets. Grey districts are an honest gap, not a zero: the crop yearbook does not have a row for every district, crop, season and year, and hovering says why instead of guessing. See Methodology inside the explorer for exactly how the data is cleaned, joined and drawn.

Where the data come from

Crop statistics

District-level area, production and yield from the DES crop yearbook, cleaned in crop_clean.ipynb. Names are matched onto the map polygons. Covers 1997–98 through 2022–23.

IMD rainfall

India Meteorological Department 0.25° daily rainfall grids for 1997–2023, downloaded and turned into district seasonal totals in imd_clean.ipynb.

Boundaries

Current district polygons in data/INDIA_DISTRICTS.geojson. Crop and rainfall are both joined onto this one map, so every district uses the same shape regardless of when the underlying statistic was published.

Use cases

Monsoon impact on yield

Colour by rainfall anomaly on one side and yield on the other, same crop/season/year, then swipe to see whether a weak or strong monsoon lines up with low or high yield in a district.

Year-over-year comparison

Same crop and season, two different years left and right — a drought year against a normal one, or the latest year against a decade ago — to see which districts moved and by how much.

Cropping pattern by district

Hold the year fixed and switch crop or season to see where a crop is actually grown and reported, versus where it is genuinely absent rather than just under-reported.

Quick reference without raw files

Skip downloading and joining the DES yearbook, IMD grids and a district shapefile — the join and its known gaps are already worked out and explained on hover.

Methodology

How the data is cleaned, joined and rendered

Crop statistics and rainfall are cleaned in separate notebooks, merged into one table, then drawn on the map exactly as that table has it — nothing is filled in to make a district look complete. This page walks through each step and every edge case it runs into.

Seasons and dates

The crop file only carries season names (Kharif, Rabi, Total, Whole Year, Summer, Winter, Autumn). Those rows are not given calendar start and end dates, so yield and sown area are never re-windowed. Rainfall uses fixed calendar windows, then the totals are labelled with the same agricultural year as the crop file (2014-2015 means the year that starts in 2014).

Season Dates used How it is labelled
Kharif 1 June through 30 September of the start year (122 days). Example for 2014-2015: 1 June 2014 – 30 September 2014. Crop: rows tagged Kharif in the CSV, as given. Rain: IMD sum over those dates.
Rabi 1 October through 31 December of the start year, plus 1 January through 31 March of the next calendar year (182 days in a non-leap year). Example for 2014-2015: 1 October 2014 – 31 March 2015. Crop: rows tagged Rabi in the CSV. Rain: IMD sum over those dates, tagged to the year Rabi started (not to January’s calendar year).
Annual (rainfall only) 1 January through 31 December of the start year (365 days, or 366 if that calendar year is a leap year). Example for 2014-2015: 1 January 2014 – 31 December 2014 — not October–March, and not June–May. Used when the crop season is anything other than Kharif or Rabi (Total, Whole Year, Summer, Winter, Autumn).
Total No calendar window. This is a reporting category in the crop yearbook, not a date range we compute. Yield/area: CSV rows labelled Total. Rain: the Annual window above.
Whole Year No calendar window in this app. Shown only if the crop file has that label. Yield/area: CSV as-is. Rain: Annual (1 Jan – 31 Dec of the start year).
Summer, Winter, Autumn No calendar windows defined here. The crop file uses these labels; we do not map them onto months. Yield/area: CSV as-is. Rain: Annual (1 Jan – 31 Dec of the start year).

April and May are not in Kharif or Rabi. They still sit inside the Annual rainfall total. Months are inclusive: Kharif includes 30 September; Rabi includes 31 March.

How the crop table is prepared

See crop_clean.ipynb. Rows missing a district, crop or year are dropped. Duplicates: yield is the mean, area and production are summed. District names are normalised onto the GeoJSON (garbled map labels, aliases like Ahmadabad/Ahmedabad). Unmatched names are listed in crop_unmatched_districts.csv and do not go on the map.

build_panel.ipynb then stacks crop rows with rain-only rows (crop left blank) so a grey yield polygon still has a rainfall row and a written reason.

How IMD rainfall is turned into districts

Each land cell (~25 km) is assigned to the district that contains its centre. Tiny districts that catch no cell use the nearest cell to their centroid. Cells in a district are averaged each day, then summed over the windows in Seasons and dates (Kharif 1 Jun–30 Sep; Rabi 1 Oct–31 Mar; Annual 1 Jan–31 Dec of the start year).

Years are labelled 1997–1998 to match the crop file. Seasons with under 90% of expected days are dropped. The anomaly is the percent difference from that district’s long-term seasonal mean. This is written by imd_clean.ipynb to data/processed/rainfall_district_seasonal.csv.

How edge cases are handled

Public crop yearbooks, IMD grids and a current district map were never designed as one table. We do not invent values to make the map look complete. Each case below is either dropped, left grey, repaired only when the match is unique, or computed from the grid onto today’s polygons (rainfall only).

No crop row for that district

If the statistics file has no area/yield for that crop, season and year, the polygon stays grey. Missing is not filled with zero, a neighbour, or a state average. The legend shows how many of the 820 polygons actually coloured.

Crop not grown or not reported everywhere

Rice, wheat and the rest are not present in every district. A grey Rice map in a non-rice district or a grey Rabi map where only Kharif is reported is expected. It does not mean the join failed.

Season labels (Total, Whole Year, Summer…)

Crop colour uses the season string from the CSV as-is — those labels have no start/end dates in the file. Total is a reporting category many states leave blank, so it is much emptier than Kharif. Whole Year, Summer, Winter and Autumn stay on the crop map with no extra date filter. For rainfall, Kharif is 1 June–30 September and Rabi is 1 October–31 March; every other crop season uses IMD Annual (1 January–31 December of the start year).

Blank or duplicate crop rows

Rows with no district, crop or year are dropped. Duplicate rows for the same district–crop–season–year: yield is the mean of the reported yields; area and production are summed.

Spelling, punctuation and “District”

Names are lowercased, & becomes “and”, punctuation is stripped, and a trailing “district” is removed so “Nashik District” and “Nashik” join. Parenthetical extra names (for example Kabirdham) are stripped and tried as a second key.

Garbled and truncated map labels

The GeoJSON file misspells many districts (Bengal#ru, B>NKURA, DEHRAD@N, B\dar). Those characters are repaired to letters before matching. Short truncated labels (for example Chikkaball) are joined only if exactly one crop-table name starts with that stem. One-letter leftovers (B, H, empty names) are left unmatched.

Known renames and aliases

A small alias list covers pairs such as Ahmadabad/Ahmedabad, Ahmednagar/Ahilyanagar, Mysuru/Mysore, Angul/Anugul, Budgam/Badgam. If two crop names are equally close, we do not guess — the polygon stays grey.

District codes are not used

The crop file’s district_code and the map’s dist_code / D_CODE are different numbering systems (the same number can mean Chittoor on the map and a district in another state in the CSV). Join is by name only, never by those codes.

New districts and splits (MAUP)

The map is today’s boundaries (~820 polygons). The crop table keeps historical names. Tirupati exists in the crop file only from 2022–23; Chittoor still has the older years. We do not copy Chittoor yield onto Tirupati. Rainfall is the opposite: every current polygon, including Tirupati, gets a zonal mean from the IMD grid in every year, so a new district can show rain in 2014 even though it had no crop row then.

Same name on several polygons

The map has more features than unique district names. If two polygons normalise to the same name, they share the same crop or rain record. That is a map-file issue, not an extra statistic.

IMD ocean and missing cells

Grid value -999 is treated as missing (ocean or no observation), not as rainfall. Only finite land cells enter the district average.

Districts smaller than a 0.25° cell

Assignment is point-in-polygon on the cell centre. If that misses, we try intersection. Tiny districts that still catch no cell take the nearest IMD cell to their centroid so they are not silently dropped from rainfall.

Incomplete rainfall seasons

A district–season is dropped if fewer than 90% of expected days are present: 122 for Kharif (1 Jun–30 Sep), 182 for Rabi (1 Oct–31 Mar), 365 for Annual (1 Jan–31 Dec), or 366 in a leap calendar year. Partial seasons are not scaled up to look complete.

Rabi across 1 January

Rabi rainfall for 2014-2015 is 1 October 2014 through 31 March 2015. January–March 2016 belongs to 2015-2016, not to 2014-2015. Kharif for 2014-2015 is only 1 June 2014 through 30 September 2014. Full windows are in Seasons and dates.

Rainfall anomaly

Anomaly is percent difference from that district’s own long-term seasonal mean in the processed file. It is not a comparison to a neighbouring district. The colour scale is centred at zero and shared by left and right, so one very wet year can stretch both sides.

Left and right colour scale

Both scenarios share one scale built from the values that exist on either side. A district missing on one side stays grey there; we do not interpolate the gap. Hover shows “No data” rather than 0.

Rain does not depend on crop

When the metric is rainfall or anomaly, crop menus are disabled. Rain does not depend on crop.

Files missing or unloadable

If the page cannot fetch the CSV or GeoJSON (file://, blocked port, missing path), it says so and offers local file pickers. It does not fall back to sample or dummy districts.

How to read the map

1 · Metric Pick what to colour

Yield, sown area, rainfall in millimetres, or rainfall anomaly. Red is drier than normal; blue is wetter.

2 · Scenarios Left vs right

Set crop, season and year on each side. For rainfall, crop is disabled — rain does not depend on crop.

3 · Slider Drag the bar

Wipe one map over the other. Pan and zoom stay locked together so you are always looking at the same place.

4 · Hover Read the numbers

Point at a district for the left value, the right value, and the difference. Both sides share one colour scale.

5 · Gaps Grey is missing

Grey means there is no crop statistics row for that district, crop, season and year — not a yield of zero. Rainfall still colours almost every polygon because it comes from the IMD grid, not the crop table. Prefer Kharif over Total if the yield map looks empty.
